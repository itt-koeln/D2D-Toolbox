## Background

Water Evaluation and Planning System (WEAP) is a water allocation and planning software that adopts an integrated approach to water resources planning and was developed by the Stockholm Environmental Institute’s U.S. Center. With time steps ranging from daily to monthly and annually, WEAP can simulate hydrologic processes and water allocation of limited water resources between agricultural, municipal, and environmental uses that require the full integration of supply, demand, water quality, and ecological considerations. It can be considered as a decision support system as it provides a comprehensive, flexible and user-friendly framework for planning and policy analysis by using the scenario development option. It also includes a hydrological model, as well as several modules that allow the integration of groundwater and water quality.

Modeling a river system using WEAP requires knowing water supply and demand volumes.

- For more information about WEAP refer to the Water Evaluation And Planning website, Available from [here](https://www.weap21.org/index.asp)

## Principle

It operates on the basic principle of a water balance and can simulate a broad range of natural and engineered components of these systems, including rainfall-runoff, baseflow, and groundwater recharge from precipitation; sectoral demand analyses; water conservation; water rights and allocation priorities, reservoir operations; hydropower generation; pollution tracking and water quality; vulnerability assessments; and ecosystem requirements. A financial module is also included, which allows cost-benefit comparisons to be made (SEI, n.d.).

## Manual

WEAP source file can be downloaded from the WEAP [website](https://www.weap21.org/index.asp?action=40) and can be installed straightforward ([here is the guideline](https://www.weap21.org/downloads/WEAP_User_Guide.pdf)).  However, a license is required to use the tool. Basic data required to develop a WEAP model include river flow, water demand, system operation policies, reservoir data, and river reaches data. The amount of additional data required depends on the mathematical models selected to simulate the physical processes and to calculate agricultural water demand for example.

WEAP presents the user with different types of basic graphical icons representing features of a river basin objects. Each object contains the data structure, required for modeling processes on that object. Each object can be connected with other objects and afterward, the model can be run.  Linear Program solution optimizes the distribution of water at each time step.
