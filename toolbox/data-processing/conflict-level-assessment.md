## Background

Conflict level assessment can be used to evaluate current degrees of conflict escalation in any given context. The assessment is based on the theory that undisturbed conflicts evolve in an escalating way of severity by passing defined stages. These processes are treated as universal and can be applied on different scales reaching from conflicting stakeholder interests to emerging resources disputes and from the local to the international level.

The conflict level assessment depends on data availability or data collection methodology such as interview-based surveys. Once the current conflict level has been assessed, it represents a stepping stone towards decision making and adequate intervention plans.

Further information on resolution tools for different conflict stages ([2)](https://th-koeln.sciebo.de/s/IVbBC7pvmEiz6gx).

## Principle

Through a conflict level assessment, the researcher can study and categorize the current status of opponent relationships concerning any given topic. The assessment investigates presence or absence of traits typical for the different conflict stages and subsequently relates a conflict stage to the investigated situation. In the current literature, different models of conflict stages exist. While the general idea behind the stages aligns, their number and preciseness differ. The assessment of conflict stages is based on social-science principles and can be seen as an inevitable tool to investigate human aspects of social-ecological systems under stress.

## Manual

The analyzed conflict needs to be understood in order to choose the appropriate conflict stage model for the conflict level assessment. Information on conflict parameters and different conflict stage models can be found ([here (1)](https://th-koeln.sciebo.de/s/IVbBC7pvmEiz6gx)).

Once the conflict stage model has been chosen data requirements need to be checked. If required data aren’t available, data collection through interviews with all involved parties is recommendable. Interviews assess the presence or absence of traits related to specific conflict stages:

Cornelius et al. (2007) present five conflict stages:

1. Uncomfortableness: an inner, intuitive feeling that something is going wrong
2. Incidents: irritation from all sides, things happen, parties get agitated
3. Misunderstanding: communication is deficient, story-lines differ
4. Tension: negative attitudes, consciously or unconsciously people hurt each other 5. Crisis: repressed emotions release. Violence can appear

Further Information on conflict questionnaires can be found ([3](https://th-koeln.sciebo.de/s/IVbBC7pvmEiz6gx)).

## Example

The assessment is a valuable tool in the context of resources conflicts. It is best applied as a stepping stone for further action. It can be used in situations where a project is facing difficulties with opposing stakeholder positions in order to find appropriate communication tools. Furthermore, it can evaluate the stage of seriousness in advanced resources conflict situations and help to choose the right diplomatic tool from negotiation to litigation.
