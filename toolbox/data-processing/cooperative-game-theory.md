# Cooperative game theory

*Posted 2018-06-21* · Category: Transboundary gaming

**Prepared by: Mahsa Motlagh**

---

**1. Background**

Game theory is "the study of mathematical models of conflict and cooperation between intelligent rational decision-makers". Game theory provides useful insights into the way parties that share a scarce resource may plan their use of the resource in different situations. This review provides a brief and self-contained introduction to the theory of cooperative games. Game theory can be applied to decision making in bargaining, voting and negotiation situations when the dynamics of sequential interactions are not fully understood.

---

**2. Principle**

The central purpose of game theory is to study the strategic relations between supposedly rational players. It thus explores the social structures within which the consequences of a player’s action depend, in a conscious way for the player, on the actions of the other players. The prehistory of game theory is relatively short, devoted to an algorithm for the resolution of extensive-form games and an equilibrium notion for zero-sum normal-form games.

---

**3. Manual**

The main models (bargaining games, transfer utility, and non-transfer utility games) and issues and solutions are considered: bargaining solutions, single-value solutions like the Shapley value and the nucleolus, and multi-value solutions such as the core.

---

**4. Example**

Game theory can deliver valuable insights into the strategic behavior of stakeholders in complex water-food-energy domains where the interactions of the stakeholders normally result in outcomes that are sub-optimal from a system’s (central)perspective. Game theory can be applied in any field where more than one actor is involved in the decision-making process and the final outcome depends on the participants’ strategic behavior, their willingness to cooperate, risk attitude, access to information, uncertainty exposure and other behavioral factors.
