## Background

A natural hydrological regime is essential for biodiversity conservation and the sustainability, structure, and functioning of aquatic ecosystems. Increasing alteration of natural flow regimes due to human activities places aquatic ecosystems under increasing risk and can lead to environmental degradation.

To be able to assign a high ecological status for water bodies, the WFD explicitly requires the assessment of hydromorphological elements.

## Principle

- Recommendation by the German Working Group on water issues of the Federal States and the Federal Government (LAWA) to classify the hydrological regime as part of the hydromorphological quality elements group under the WFD.

- WFD-compliant and pressure-based approach

- Uses data standards: WFD-Codelist, PressureTypeCode, and Quality Status Code

- The assessment is conducted for every individual WFD-water-body through calculation methods using GIS-based and quantifiable data sets with defined class limits. In case of insufficient data availability, an expert assessment is carried out.

- A direct derivation of management measures regarding the hydrological regime is possible.

## Manual

- Classifies the anthropogenic pressures into 6 comprehensive pressure groups in terms of key features of anthropogenic interference of the hydrological regime. Within these pressure groups, at least one criterion has to be assessed after Annex V WFD.
