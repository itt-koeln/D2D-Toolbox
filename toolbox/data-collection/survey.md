# Survey

*Posted 2018-06-21*

**Prepared by: Mahsa Motlagh**

---

**1. Background**

Survey methodology studies the sampling of individual units from a population and the associated survey data collection techniques, such as questionnaire construction and methods for improving the number and accuracy of responses to surveys.

The most popular variations of surveys include questionnaires, interviews and documentation review.

---

**2. Principle**

In natural resources and environmental management, the multiplicity of human value-judgment urges to develop new types of techniques helping decision maker(s) to make the best decisions.

This approach has three phases: (a) survey design, (b) survey instrument development, and (c) survey execution. Data analysis and reporting of results are identified as an important fourth phase of the survey process.

---

**3. Manual**

Surveys offer the opportunity to execute studies with various designs, each of which is suitable for addressing particular research questions of long-standing interest of each study.

- Establish the goals of the research
- Determine the sample group
- Choose the interviewing methodology
- Create questionnaire
- Pre-test the questionnaire, if practical
- Conduct interviews and enter data
- Analyze the data - Produce the reports

---

**4. Example**

Researchers, social psychologists and decision makers are interested in understanding how people influence and are influenced by, their social environment. Available survey solutions have led to widespread use of quantitative surveys, across all survey modes, to collect, analyze, and use data to formulate strategies for a more effective management model and create solution-based projections for effective policymaking and regulation.
